01The strategy

A systematic approach to a changing market

HERAKLIT is a rules-based Nasdaq-100 exposure strategy. It adjusts the share of the portfolio invested in a daily leveraged Nasdaq-100 exchange traded fund and holds any uninvested capital in cash. It is not a discretionary stock-selection portfolio and does not depend on choosing individual technology-company winners.

Markets change. A long-term investment strategy should have a disciplined way to respond.

The strategy works on two coordinated decision frequencies: a scheduled risk-regime review every three US trading sessions and a daily recalculation of portfolio exposure. The risk model decides whether a position is permitted; observed volatility governs its size. An empirical state-transition assessment, conceptually motivated by the Architecture of Distinction, adds a conditional adjustment based on the estimated direction of the market state. FRAMONT & Partners Management Ltd applies these rules in managing the segregated portfolio.

Four principles
  • a

    Nasdaq growth exposure

    Seek participation in the long-term development of the Nasdaq-100 through a rules-based, dynamically sized position.

  • b

    Risk-aware allocation

    Use estimated downside risk and observed volatility to decide when market exposure should be reduced, increased or replaced with cash.

  • c

    A view of market transitions

    Analyse the probability of entering a stressed market state and the estimated direction in which the current state is evolving.

  • d

    Consistency over intuition

    Apply documented decision rules at defined market closes rather than emotional reactions to daily headlines.

These principles describe intent. They are not a promise, a target return or a guarantee. The model can misjudge market conditions and the strategy can lose value in any environment, including when the Nasdaq rises.

03The science behind HERAKLIT

The Architecture of Distinction

The Architecture of Distinction (AoD), presented by Marcus Schmieke in his 2026 book The Architecture of Distinction: How Quantum Theory, Spacetime, and Consciousness Unfold from a Single Act, is a theoretical programme that begins with the act of distinction and investigates the emergence of structure, dynamics and coherence. The book develops what Schmieke calls the Mother Equation, a general mathematical formulation of change articulated through drift, diffusion and circulation. These concepts form the intellectual foundation of HERAKLIT Strategy.

The Mother Equation, in accessible terms

In AoD the three components play different roles in a changing system: stabilising movement towards preferred configurations, spreading under uncertainty, and circulation that preserves non-gradient dynamics. The framework is a research proposal, not an established description of financial-market behaviour.

1

Drift

In AoD: directional movement associated with a changing tension landscape. Careful investment analogy: estimating whether the local market-trend state tends to improve or deteriorate.

2

Diffusion

In AoD: the spreading of a density while uncertainty remains active. Careful investment analogy: recognising volatility and dispersion as central to exposure management.

3

Circulation

In AoD: a non-gradient, potentially recurrent current within a state space. Careful investment analogy: remaining open to recurrent and non-linear market dynamics. The current trading rule does not identify this term separately.

In mathematical form, AoD presents the Mother Equation as a continuity law for a possibility density, ∂ρ/∂λ = −∇·J, with the current J = −D∇ρ − ρ∇Φ + ρΩ for diffusion, drift and circulation. The source-space parameter λ is not simply financial calendar time.

From philosophical inspiration to an empirical trading rule

HERAKLIT Strategy connects this theoretical perspective with observable financial data rather than replacing market evidence with abstract physics. It describes market conditions in a state space formed by a Nasdaq price-trend coordinate and the VIX, then estimates how those states move between different regions of that space.

Two quantities play a specific role in portfolio decisions. The committor Q estimates the probability of reaching a defined “danger” region before a defined “safe” region. A local trend-drift estimate summarises the expected direction of the trend coordinate under the same empirical transition model. The additional position multiplier applies only when Q is at least 0.5 and the modelled trend drift is positive.

A clarification that matters

The Mother Equation supplies a conceptual architecture for thinking about dynamic states and probability flows. The investment signals themselves are estimated from historical financial data with a Markov transition model: the complete AoD equation is not fitted as a confirmed physical law of market prices. The committor is only one observable of a transition model, and many different models can share it; the trend drift is estimated from financial data, not derived from AoD axioms. The simulated historical performance does not show that AoD causes investment returns, does not prove the Mother Equation as a law of markets and cannot validate claims about quantum measurement or consciousness.

04The model

From transition probabilities to portfolio decisions

HERAKLIT combines three interacting elements: a statistical downside-risk regime, volatility-based position sizing and a state-transition model. A high estimated probability of visiting a stressed market state does not by itself lead to more investment: an additional multiplier is considered only if the local trend drift is positive and the risk regime already permits an invested position.

The risk model decides whether a position is permitted. Observed volatility decides its size.

01 Observe: price, stress and volatility

Daily adjusted Nasdaq proxy prices and the VIX are the inputs for a statistical description of market conditions. One component estimates the risk that the Nasdaq proxy falls by at least 10% within the following 20 trading sessions.

02 Classify: participate or reduce exposure

At each scheduled decision point, every three US trading sessions, the model assesses whether conditions support a risk-on posture or call for a risk-off allocation. Separate exit and re-entry thresholds provide hysteresis, so the portfolio does not switch at every small fluctuation.

03 Evaluate: the direction within the transition

A second model estimates a committor, the probability of reaching a predefined stressed market region before returning to a predefined safer one, and the local direction of change of the market's trend coordinate.

04 Allocate: size the exposure, not the narrative

When the risk-regime model permits investment, the allocation moves inversely to observed 20-day volatility against a 34.65% annualised volatility budget. If the committor is at least 0.5 and the modelled trend drift is positive, the target weight is multiplied by 1.5, capped at 100% of the portfolio in the ETF.

05 Rebalance: keep rules and execution separate

The ETF weight is recalculated daily with the latest available data. The historical model assumes that a signal observed at a US market close is executed no earlier than the following US close; actual execution may differ.

What the committor measures

It asks a first-passage question: from the current state, which predefined region is likely to be reached first, a stressed one or a safer one? A high value describes a relative hitting probability, not the timing of a fall, its size or the direction of the next daily returns, and it is not the probability that the Nasdaq will fall tomorrow. A high committor may describe a market that is already stressed.

The conditional trend drift

The model estimates the expected change in the standardised trend coordinate over five trading sessions, given the present state. Its sign works as a directional filter: positive drift can allow a higher allocation in a high-committor state, but only when the risk-regime model independently permits investment. The signal may be wrong and can magnify losses.

Rules, limits and changes

FRAMONT applies the rules in managing the portfolio. A model can misjudge market conditions, react late or rest on relationships that stop holding, and each of these can produce losses. Parameters may be revised; a revision of the strategy is subject to notice to investors under the issuer's terms.

How to read the backtests

Data and period

The simulation uses daily adjusted QQQ prices, daily VIX closes and adjusted TQQQ prices; a Nasdaq-100 index series supports the state-model training for the years before QQQ existed. The active simulation covers 7 January 2002 to 22 September 2026; the extended display begins with a QQQ-only period on 10 March 1999.

Execution and costs

Decisions use information up to the relevant US close and are executed at a later US close. A cost of 0.10% is charged on the capital actually reallocated, and cash earns no interest. The simulation does not deduct the instrument's costs (ongoing costs of 4.50% a year per the KID and the 20% performance fee above the high-water mark), nor issuer charges, market impact, liquidity constraints or taxes: these would reduce the results, and the instrument's costs would reduce them materially.

Currency

All figures are calculated in US dollars. The instrument is denominated in euro: for a euro investor the result would also have depended on the EUR/USD exchange rate, which can increase or reduce returns.

Retrospective specification

The parameters were defined with access to past market data. This can introduce hindsight and selection bias and may materially overstate future performance, so the test period is not an independent, real-time validation of the rules. Stress scenarios, alternative indicators, simulated execution delays and cost sensitivity checks reveal limitations, but cannot replace a long prospective observation period, an independent operational audit or genuinely unseen data.

Why a 79% drawdown is shown

The 1999–2026 line includes the QQQ-only period during the dot-com decline, with a peak-to-trough loss of about 79.14%. The active simulation from January 2002 has a separately calculated maximum drawdown of about 63.28%. Both losses are hypothetical, and future losses could be greater.

Comparing with QQQ

QQQ is a useful unleveraged reference for Nasdaq-100 participation, but not a risk-matched comparison for a strategy that can hold TQQQ, which seeks a multiple of daily index returns. TQQQ's daily reset can produce outcomes very different from a simple fixed multiple over weeks, months and years.

09Risk disclosure

Read these before you go further

A summary of the principal risks, not a complete list. The Key Information Document and the prospectus contain the full disclosure. Risks and benefits are given equal prominence on this page.

R1Capital loss

There is no capital protection and no guaranteed return. You can lose part or all of the amount invested.

R2Drawdowns

The simulated active strategy lost more than 60% from peak to trough, and the full simulation including the QQQ-only period shows a loss near 79%. Future losses may exceed these figures, up to the total loss of the amount invested.

R3Daily leverage and path dependency

The ETF used by the strategy targets three times the daily return of the Nasdaq-100. Over longer periods compounding and volatility can produce returns very different from a constant multiple of the index, and a 100% allocation creates economic exposure to the Nasdaq-100 of about three times the portfolio. The mandate allows total exposure of up to 300%. Leverage amplifies losses as well as gains.

R4Model and estimation risk

The signal relies on statistical choices, historical relationships and defined state boundaries. Structural market changes, unstable transition estimates, data corrections and model failures may lead to decisions that worsen returns, including losses or missed gains.

R5Simulation and research selection

The long-term returns are backtests whose parameters were chosen with knowledge of past market behaviour, which can produce selection bias and over-fitting. They are not a verified real-money track record, are not the instrument's performance and do not indicate future results. Before February 2010 the leveraged history is synthetic.

R6Concentration

Exposure is concentrated in Nasdaq-100 growth companies, which can be vulnerable to sector-specific revaluations and to changes in interest rates, valuations and market sentiment.

R7Execution

Signals computed from closing prices may not be executable at the assumed prices. Spreads, illiquidity, exchange interruptions, order limits and delayed execution can materially reduce returns.

R8Short positions

The mandate allows short positions in shares, ETFs and currencies, although the current rules do not use them. A short position carries a theoretically unlimited potential loss.

R9Currency risk

The instrument is in euro, but Nasdaq-linked underlyings trade in dollars. Exchange rate moves affect the value, positively or negatively.

R10Reduced exposure phases

When the model cuts exposure, the portfolio may sit out of the market while it rises. The opportunity cost is part of the strategy.

R11Short instrument history

The instrument has traded since 26 May 2026. Its history is too short to draw conclusions about the strategy's behaviour in live conditions.

R12Dependence on the developer and the model

The strategy rests on a proprietary model developed by Marcus Schmieke. The unavailability of the developer, the model or the data feeding it affects the strategy. A change of strategy is subject to notice under the issuer's terms.

R13Liquidity and trading

In unusual market conditions or technical disruptions, buying and selling may be temporarily hindered or impossible and the bid-ask spread may widen.

R14Issuer and early redemption

The instrument is a debt security of the issuer, collateralised but not covered by any deposit protection scheme. The issuer may redeem it at its discretion with notice, including at a time unfavourable to the investor.

R15Conflicts of interest

The performance fee creates an incentive to take risk. The safeguard lies in the mandate limits and in the oversight of the manager and the issuer.

R16Costs

Fees, issuer charges and trading costs reduce the return, and the part not linked to performance is payable in negative periods too. The KID shows their impact.

R17Regulation and tax

Changes in law, regulation or tax treatment can affect the instrument, its structure or the net return to the investor.

The portfolio follows the systematic HERAKLIT strategy developed by Marcus Schmieke, which allocates between a daily leveraged Nasdaq-100 ETF and cash. Capital is at risk.

How does the strategy differ from a conventional Nasdaq investment?

Unlike a permanent index holding, the strategy can reduce its Nasdaq exposure or move into cash when its risk model indicates more challenging conditions. While invested, it adjusts the size of its position in a daily leveraged Nasdaq-100 ETF every day.

How does the strategy set its exposure?

When the risk regime permits investing, the target weight in the ETF is derived from a 34.65% annualised volatility budget and observed 20-day volatility. It is multiplied by 1.5 only when the estimated committor is at least 0.5 and the modelled local trend drift is positive, and it is always capped at 100% of the portfolio. Actual portfolio volatility can differ considerably from the budget.

How often does the strategy trade?

The risk-regime decision and the conditional allocation factor are reviewed every third US trading session on a fixed market-calendar schedule, while the ETF target weight is recalculated every day. A three-session review cycle therefore does not mean that the portfolio trades only once every three days.

Does it always invest when the market is rising?

No. The estimated loss-risk regime can keep the portfolio in cash even during parts of a recovery. The historical simulations include both missed opportunities and well-timed reductions.

What does the committor measure?

It is the estimated probability of reaching a predefined stressed market region before a safer one, under a transition model fitted to historical data. It is not the probability that the Nasdaq will fall tomorrow.

Why can exposure increase when the committor is high?

The 1.5 multiplier is used only when the risk-classification model permits an invested position and the transition model indicates positive trend drift. This combination can add exposure during stress or recovery, but the signal may be wrong and can magnify losses.

Is the strategy systematic or discretionary?

It is systematic. Decisions follow rules set in advance and empirically calibrated, applied by FRAMONT & Partners Management Ltd in managing the segregated portfolio. A model can nonetheless misread a market phase or react late: being systematic does not remove the risk of loss.

How does the strategy relate to the Architecture of Distinction?

HERAKLIT Strategy is based conceptually on the Architecture of Distinction, the theoretical framework developed by Marcus Schmieke, and on its Mother Equation, which describes change through drift, diffusion and circulation. The tradable signals, however, are estimated empirically from market data with a statistical transition model: the strategy does not establish the Mother Equation as a physical law of markets, and its results do not prove the theory.

Is HERAKLIT a quantum-trading system?

No. Its tradable decisions rest on conventional market observations and empirical statistical models. The Architecture of Distinction provides a conceptual inspiration and a mathematical vocabulary; the backtest does not demonstrate a quantum advantage.

Does the Mother Equation guarantee profits?

No. The Mother Equation is part of the proposed Architecture of Distinction research programme. The market model does not establish that the equation is valid as a physical or economic law of markets, and nothing on this page guarantees a return.

Are the returns shown real results?

No. They are hypothetical backtest simulations through 22 September 2026. The 27.01% compound annual growth rate refers to the active strategy from 7 January 2002; the 23.02% rate refers to the full history from 10 March 1999, which includes an unleveraged QQQ-only period through 2001 and synthetic leveraged returns before TQQQ existed. Both are in US dollars, before the instrument's costs, do not represent actual trading and are not a reliable indicator of future results. The instrument has traded since 26 May 2026 and its price is published by the issuer.

What is the maximum drawdown?

In the active simulation the worst peak-to-trough loss was about 63.28%, during the 2007–2009 financial crisis. Including the QQQ-only period from 1999 it was about 79.14%, largely because of the dot-com decline before active trading began in January 2002. Actual losses could be larger.

Is the historical performance independently audited or live-tested?

No. The figures are research backtests using historical public data, specified modelling assumptions and reconstructed transaction rules. They are not independently audited investment returns and are not supported by an independently observed live track record of the strategy.

Can the strategy lose money in market crises?

Yes. The historical simulation suffered very large losses in crisis periods, including 2008. Conditional leverage and changes in risk exposure provide no floor on portfolio value.

What is TQQQ?

TQQQ (ProShares UltraPro QQQ) is a US-listed exchange traded fund that seeks three times the daily return of the Nasdaq-100 index. Because it targets a multiple of daily, not multi-year, returns, its longer-term performance depends on the path of market prices and on realised volatility.
