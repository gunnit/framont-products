01The strategy

Investing in growth, with a gauge of market stability

HERAKLIT does not try to forecast the next move of the Nasdaq. It measures instead whether the market is in a stable phase, in which exposure to growth is sustainable, or in an unstable phase, in which risk must be reduced. The model turns that reading into defined rules that govern how much the portfolio is exposed.

A model that decides how much to be exposed, not a manager guessing the direction.

Decisions are not discretionary: they follow rules set in advance and empirically calibrated, applied by FRAMONT & Partners Management Ltd in managing the segregated portfolio. The model was calibrated and verified on historical series, which is precisely why the long-term results attached to it are simulations, not realised performance.

01Investment objective
  • a

    Participate in Nasdaq growth

    Take exposure to the growth potential of Nasdaq-linked securities in the phases the model classifies as stable and investable.

  • b

    Reduce exposure in unstable phases

    Assess risk systematically and move exposure towards defensive positioning, up to 100% cash, when the detected regime is not favourable.

  • c

    Dynamic allocation

    Hold no fixed weights: size exposure according to the detected market regime, within the limits of the mandate.

Objectives describe intent. They are not a promise, a target return or a guarantee. The model can misclassify a market phase and the strategy can lose value in any environment, including when the Nasdaq rises.

03The science behind HERAKLIT

Inspired by the Architecture of Distinction

HERAKLIT Strategy draws its conceptual inspiration from the Architecture of Distinction (AoD), a theoretical framework developed by Marcus Schmieke. At its heart is the Mother Equation, which describes the evolution of complex systems through three fundamental processes: drift, diffusion and circulation. These principles provide a conceptual foundation for understanding market trends, volatility and changing market dynamics.

Three processes, one reading of the market

The Mother Equation breaks the change of a complex system into three components. Applied to markets, they offer a vocabulary to tell apart what is trend, what is noise and what signals a change of regime.

1

Drift

The directional component of movement: the underlying tendency along which the system moves over time.

2

Diffusion

The random dispersion around the tendency: the width of the fluctuations, which in markets reads as volatility.

3

Circulation

The recirculating flows that are neither trend nor noise: the internal dynamics that change the structure of the system and, in markets, the regime.

From theory to rules

HERAKLIT Strategy translates this perspective into a practical, rules-based investment approach, combining the growth potential of the Nasdaq with systematic risk assessment and dynamic portfolio allocation.

The theoretical framework is set out in Marcus Schmieke's book The Architecture of Distinction (2026), which proposes a frame for understanding how patterns, structure and change emerge in complex systems.

A clarification that matters

The investment model is empirically calibrated and historically backtested. It is not mathematically derived directly from the Mother Equation. The theory provides the inspiration and the vocabulary; the rules governing the portfolio are the outcome of a calibration on data, with the limits that any calibration on the past carries.

04The model

Regime detection across twelve data streams

The model applies a market regime detection derived from the Fokker–Planck equation, the physics equation that describes how the probability distribution of a system subject to drift and diffusion evolves over time. Fed by twelve global data streams, it separates stable, investable phases from unstable ones and adjusts the portfolio's exposure systematically.

The question the model answers is not "where is the market going" but "how stable is it right now".

Stable phase

When the data point to a stable regime, the portfolio holds or increases its exposure to Nasdaq growth, within the limits of the mandate.

Unstable phase

When the data point to an unstable regime, exposure is reduced towards defensive positioning, down to cash. The aim is to contain losses, not to avoid them altogether.

Rules, not judgement

The model's signals are translated into predefined rules applied by FRAMONT in managing the portfolio. Parameters are empirically calibrated and may be revised; a revision of the strategy is subject to notice to investors under the issuer's terms.

Twelve global data streams

The model reads twelve global data streams to classify the market regime. The list of streams, their weights and the model's parameters are proprietary and are not published on this page.

Dynamic, not static, exposure

The portfolio does not passively track an index. Exposure changes with the detected regime and may stay reduced for extended periods, even while the Nasdaq rises.

The limits of any model

A model can misclassify a market phase, react late to a regime change or be calibrated on a past that does not repeat. None of these outcomes is excluded, and each can produce losses.

09Risk disclosure

Read these before you go further

A summary of the principal risks, not a complete list. The Key Information Document and the prospectus contain the full disclosure. Risks and benefits are given equal prominence on this page.

R1Capital loss

There is no capital protection and no guaranteed return. You can lose part or all of the amount invested.

R2Model risk

The strategy depends on a model that can misclassify the market regime, react late to a change or rely on relationships that stop holding. A model error produces losses or missed gains.

R3Simulated results

The long-term returns are backtests calculated after the fact and subject to over-fitting. They are not the instrument's performance and do not indicate future results.

R4Concentration

Growth exposure is concentrated in Nasdaq-linked and technology securities. A negative phase in that segment directly affects the result.

R5Leverage and complex products

The portfolio may hold leveraged exchange traded products and the mandate allows total exposure of up to 300%. Leverage amplifies losses as well as gains.

R6Short positions

The mandate allows short positions in shares, ETFs and currencies. A short position carries a theoretically unlimited potential loss.

R7Currency risk

The instrument is in euro, but Nasdaq-linked underlyings trade in dollars. Exchange rate moves affect the value, positively or negatively.

R8Reduced exposure phases

When the model cuts exposure, the portfolio may sit out of the market while it rises. The opportunity cost is part of the strategy.

R9Short instrument history

The instrument has traded since 26 May 2026. Its history is too short to draw conclusions about the strategy's behaviour in live conditions.

R10Dependence on the developer and the model

The strategy rests on a proprietary model developed by Marcus Schmieke. The unavailability of the developer, the model or the data feeding it affects the strategy. A change of strategy is subject to notice under the issuer's terms.

R11Liquidity and trading

In unusual market conditions or technical disruptions, buying and selling may be temporarily hindered or impossible and the bid-ask spread may widen.

R12Issuer and early redemption

The instrument is a debt security of the issuer, collateralised but not covered by any deposit protection scheme. The issuer may redeem it at its discretion with notice, including at a time unfavourable to the investor.

R13Conflicts of interest

The performance fee creates an incentive to take risk. The safeguard lies in the mandate limits and in the oversight of the manager and the issuer.

R14Costs

Fees, issuer charges and trading costs reduce the return, and the part not linked to performance is payable in negative periods too. The KID shows their impact.

R15Regulation and tax

Changes in law, regulation or tax treatment can affect the instrument, its structure or the net return to the investor.

What does market regime detection mean?

The model does not forecast the direction of the market: it measures whether the market is in a stable phase, in which exposure to growth is sustainable, or in an unstable phase, in which risk must be reduced. The detection is derived from the Fokker–Planck equation and fed by twelve global data streams. The portfolio's exposure is adjusted accordingly under rules set in advance.

Is the strategy systematic or discretionary?

It is systematic. Decisions follow rules set in advance and empirically calibrated, applied by FRAMONT & Partners Management Ltd in managing the segregated portfolio. A model can nonetheless misread a market phase or react late: being systematic does not remove the risk of loss.

How does the strategy relate to the Architecture of Distinction?

HERAKLIT Strategy draws its conceptual inspiration from the Architecture of Distinction, the theoretical framework developed by Marcus Schmieke, and in particular from the Mother Equation, which describes the evolution of complex systems through drift, diffusion and circulation. The investment model, however, is empirically calibrated and historically backtested: it is not mathematically derived directly from the Mother Equation.

Who developed the strategy and who manages the instrument?
